# Child Malnutrition

## Recognizing severe acute malnutrition (SAM)
Visible severe wasting (very thin arms/legs, loose skin folds), bilateral pitting edema (swelling)
of both feet, or a mid-upper arm circumference (MUAC) below the severe threshold for age are signs
of severe acute malnutrition. These children need urgent referral to a nutrition rehabilitation
facility or hospital, as they are at high risk of complications.

## Moderate acute malnutrition (MAM)
Children who are thin but without the severe danger signs above may be managed with supplementary
feeding and close growth monitoring at the Sub-Centre/Anganwadi level, with referral if they do not
improve or develop danger signs.

## General feeding advice
Encourage age-appropriate feeding: exclusive breastfeeding until 6 months, then timely introduction
of nutrient-dense complementary foods alongside continued breastfeeding up to 2 years. Frequent
small meals are better tolerated by malnourished children than large infrequent ones.

## When to refer immediately
Any malnourished child who also shows danger signs (unable to feed, lethargy, convulsions, severe
dehydration, or breathing difficulty) needs emergency referral regardless of the malnutrition
grading above.
