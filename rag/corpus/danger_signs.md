# General Danger Signs (Immediate Referral)

## Unconsciousness or convulsions
A patient who is unconscious, unusually drowsy and hard to wake, or has convulsions (fits/seizures)
needs immediate emergency referral to the nearest hospital or CHC. Do not attempt to give anything
by mouth. Keep the patient on their side to prevent choking, and arrange transport immediately.

## Inability to drink or feed
A patient who is unable to drink or breastfeed, or who vomits everything they are given, is showing
a danger sign and needs urgent referral. This applies especially to infants and young children.

## Severe bleeding
Any severe or uncontrolled bleeding, from any site, is an emergency. Apply firm direct pressure to
the bleeding site with a clean cloth while arranging immediate transport to the nearest facility with
surgical/emergency capability.

## Blue or grey skin color (cyanosis)
Bluish or greyish discoloration of the lips, tongue, or skin indicates the body is not getting enough
oxygen. This is always an emergency requiring immediate referral, regardless of other symptoms.

## Severe chest indrawing or grunting (children)
In young children, the chest wall pulling in sharply with each breath, or audible grunting sounds
while breathing, indicates severe respiratory distress and needs immediate referral.
