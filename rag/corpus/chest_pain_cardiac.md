# Chest Pain and Possible Cardiac Emergency

## High-risk chest pain features
Chest pain described as pressure, tightness, or heaviness — especially if it radiates to the left
arm, jaw, or back, or is accompanied by sweating, nausea, or breathlessness — should be treated as
a possible cardiac emergency and referred immediately to the nearest hospital with emergency care.

## Immediate steps while arranging transport
Keep the patient calm and seated or in a comfortable half-reclined position. Loosen tight clothing.
Do not make the patient walk unnecessarily or exert themselves. Arrange the fastest available
transport to a hospital or CHC with emergency capability; do not wait to see if the pain resolves.

## Lower-risk chest discomfort
Sharp, brief chest pain that changes with body position or breathing, without sweating,
radiation, or breathlessness, is less likely to be cardiac in origin but should still be assessed
by a Sub-Centre or ANM if it persists, to rule out other causes.
