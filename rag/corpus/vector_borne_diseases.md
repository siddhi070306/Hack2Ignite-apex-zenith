# Vector-Borne Diseases (Malaria, Dengue, Chikungunya)

## Suspected malaria
Fever with chills and sweating, often cyclical, especially in a malaria-endemic area, should be
tested for malaria at the earliest opportunity. Refer for prompt testing and treatment; delayed
treatment risks progression to severe malaria (confusion, convulsions, severe anemia, jaundice),
which is a medical emergency.

## Suspected dengue
High fever with severe headache, pain behind the eyes, joint/muscle pain, and rash should raise
suspicion of dengue. Warning signs requiring immediate referral include: severe abdominal pain,
persistent vomiting, bleeding from gums/nose, black stools, restlessness, or cold/clammy skin —
these indicate possible dengue hemorrhagic complications.

## Suspected chikungunya
High fever with severe joint pain and swelling, often affecting hands, wrists, ankles, and feet, is
typical of chikungunya. It is rarely life-threatening but can cause prolonged joint pain; supportive
care with rest, fluids, and monitoring is generally appropriate, with referral if fever is very high
or persistent beyond a few days.

## General prevention advice
Advise use of mosquito nets, elimination of standing water near the home, and use of repellents
where available, regardless of which specific illness is suspected.
