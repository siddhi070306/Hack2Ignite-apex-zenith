# Newborn Care

## Newborn danger signs
Any of the following in a baby under 28 days old needs immediate referral: not feeding at all,
convulsions, very fast or very slow breathing, severe chest indrawing, temperature that feels very
hot or very cold to touch, yellow discoloration of skin/eyes (jaundice) especially in the first 24
hours or spreading to arms/legs, or the baby is unusually floppy or unresponsive.

## Cord care
Keep the umbilical cord stump clean and dry. Do not apply any substances, oils, or powders to the
cord. Fold the diaper below the cord stump. Seek care if the cord area becomes red, swollen, or
starts discharging pus or has a foul smell.

## Warmth and breastfeeding
Keep the newborn warm with skin-to-skin contact and appropriate wrapping, especially in the first
hours after birth. Encourage exclusive breastfeeding from within the first hour of life, with no
other foods or liquids for the first six months, unless a health worker advises otherwise.

## Low birth weight babies
Babies born very small or premature need extra warmth (skin-to-skin/kangaroo care), more frequent
breastfeeding, and closer monitoring for danger signs. Refer for specialist newborn care if
available.
