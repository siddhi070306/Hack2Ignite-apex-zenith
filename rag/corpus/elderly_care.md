# Elderly Care

## Falls in the elderly
A fall in an elderly person, even without obvious injury, warrants careful assessment — check for
inability to stand/walk normally, severe pain, confusion, or head injury. Any of these needs
referral, as older adults are at higher risk of fractures and internal injury from falls.

## Confusion or sudden behavior change
Sudden new confusion, drowsiness, or agitation in an elderly person can indicate an underlying
infection (e.g. urinary tract infection), medication side effect, dehydration, or a more serious
condition like stroke. This is not a normal part of aging and should be assessed promptly rather
than dismissed.

## Managing multiple chronic conditions
Many elderly patients take multiple medications for chronic conditions. Encourage them to keep an
up-to-date list of all medications and bring it to every health visit, and to never stop a
prescribed medication without medical advice.

## Pressure sores (bedsores)
In bedridden elderly patients, check regularly for red or broken skin over bony areas (heels,
hips, lower back). Reposition the patient every 2 hours where possible, and keep skin clean and
dry. Refer if a sore is deep, infected, or not healing.
