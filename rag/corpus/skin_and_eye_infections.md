# Skin and Eye Infections

## Common skin infections
Redness, warmth, swelling, and pus in a localized area of skin suggest a bacterial skin infection.
Keep the area clean and dry, and refer if the redness is spreading rapidly, the patient has fever,
or the area is very painful — these suggest a more serious infection needing prompt attention.

## Scabies and lice
Intense itching, especially at night, with small burrows or bumps between fingers, wrists, or waist
suggests scabies. This is manageable with treatment from a health worker; encourage treating all
household members simultaneously and washing bedding/clothing to prevent reinfection.

## Conjunctivitis (red eye)
Redness, discharge, and irritation in one or both eyes without vision loss is usually simple
conjunctivitis. Advise gentle cleaning with clean water and avoiding touching/rubbing the eyes.
Refer if there is significant pain, vision changes, marked light sensitivity, or the discharge
persists beyond a few days.

## Eye injury or foreign body
Do not attempt to remove an embedded foreign object from the eye. Cover loosely and refer
immediately. Any chemical splash to the eye should be rinsed with clean water for several minutes
before urgent referral.
