# Respiratory Distress

## Recognizing breathing difficulty
Fast breathing, visible effort with each breath, chest indrawing, wheezing, or the patient
struggling to speak full sentences due to breathlessness are all signs of respiratory distress.
This is considered a high-risk symptom, particularly when it comes on suddenly or is worsening.

## When it is an emergency (Red)
Severe breathlessness at rest, chest indrawing, grunting, blue/grey lips or skin, or breathlessness
combined with chest pain or altered consciousness require immediate emergency referral and
transport to a facility capable of oxygen support.

## Moderate respiratory symptoms (Yellow)
Mild breathlessness that improves with rest, a persistent cough without other danger signs, or
breathlessness in a patient with a known chronic condition (like asthma) who has their usual
inhaler available may be manageable with ANM/Sub-Centre assessment within a few hours, with close
monitoring for worsening.

## First-aid while arranging transport
Keep the patient sitting upright rather than lying flat, loosen tight clothing, and keep them calm.
Do not give food or water if breathlessness is severe, due to choking risk.
