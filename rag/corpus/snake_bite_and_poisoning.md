# Snake Bite and Poisoning

## Snake bite first aid
Keep the patient calm and still to slow venom spread. Immobilize the bitten limb, keeping it below
heart level if possible. Remove tight clothing/jewellery near the bite before swelling starts. Do
NOT cut the wound, apply a tourniquet, try to suck out venom, or apply ice. Arrange immediate
transport to a facility that stocks anti-snake-venom.

## Danger signs after a snake bite
Difficulty breathing, drooping eyelids or blurred vision, difficulty swallowing, bleeding from
gums/nose, dark urine, or spreading swelling are all signs of severe envenomation requiring
emergency transport, even if the patient initially looks well.

## Suspected poisoning (ingestion)
Identify what was ingested if possible and bring the container/substance to the facility. Do not
induce vomiting unless specifically advised by a health professional, as some substances cause
more harm coming back up. Keep the patient calm and arrange immediate transport.

## Insect and scorpion stings
Most insect stings can be managed with a cold compress and monitoring. Refer immediately if there
is difficulty breathing, facial/throat swelling, widespread rash, or dizziness — these may indicate
a severe allergic reaction.
