# Tuberculosis (TB)

## Recognizing possible TB
Cough lasting more than 2 weeks, especially with blood-streaked sputum, unexplained weight loss,
persistent low-grade fever (often worse in the evening), and night sweats are classic warning signs
of pulmonary tuberculosis. Any patient with these symptoms should be referred for sputum testing or
chest X-ray at the earliest opportunity.

## Referral and testing
Refer suspected TB cases to the nearest facility offering free TB testing (sputum microscopy or
molecular testing) under the national TB program. Early diagnosis significantly improves outcomes
and reduces transmission risk to household members.

## Supporting a diagnosed TB patient
Encourage strict adherence to the full course of treatment, even after symptoms improve, since
incomplete treatment risks drug-resistant TB. Advise good ventilation at home and covering the
mouth while coughing to reduce household transmission risk.

## When TB symptoms become an emergency
Coughing up large amounts of blood, severe breathlessness, or high fever with confusion in a known
or suspected TB patient requires urgent referral to a hospital.
