# Maternal Health (Pregnancy and Postpartum)

## Danger signs during pregnancy
Severe headache with blurred vision, swelling of the face/hands, high blood pressure, vaginal
bleeding, severe abdominal pain, reduced or absent fetal movement, high fever, or convulsions in a
pregnant woman are all emergencies requiring immediate referral to a facility with obstetric care.

## Labor danger signs
Prolonged labor (more than 12 hours), heavy bleeding before or after delivery, retained placenta,
cord prolapse, or the baby not descending normally all require urgent referral to a hospital with
delivery/surgical capability.

## Postpartum danger signs
Heavy bleeding after delivery (soaking more than one pad per hour), high fever, foul-smelling
vaginal discharge, severe abdominal pain, or convulsions within 6 weeks after delivery are
emergencies requiring immediate referral.

## Routine antenatal care
Encourage regular antenatal check-ups, iron/folic acid supplementation as prescribed by a health
worker, tetanus immunization per schedule, and a balanced diet. Advise the woman to plan delivery
at a health facility rather than at home where possible.
