# Fever and Dehydration

## Assessing fever severity
A fever alone, without other danger signs, is usually manageable at home or with a Sub-Centre/ANM
visit. Fever accompanied by lethargy, inability to feed, convulsions, or persistent high fever
(above 102°F / 39°C) for more than 2-3 days should be referred for further assessment.

## Home care for mild fever
Keep the patient in a cool, well-ventilated area. Encourage plenty of fluids (water, buttermilk,
coconut water). Use a damp cloth to sponge the skin if the fever feels very high. Monitor
temperature and general alertness over the next 24 hours.

## Signs of dehydration
Look for: sunken eyes, dry mouth/tongue, reduced or dark urine, skin that stays pinched when
gently pulled (poor skin turgor), and unusual thirst or lethargy. Any of these signs, especially
in a child, indicate at least moderate dehydration.

## Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS)
For diarrhea, vomiting, or suspected dehydration without danger signs, give ORS solution
frequently in small sips — roughly one cup after every loose stool for an adult, or a few spoonfuls
every few minutes for an infant. Continue feeding/breastfeeding as normal alongside ORS. If the
patient cannot keep ORS down, or dehydration signs worsen, refer to the Sub-Centre or CHC.
