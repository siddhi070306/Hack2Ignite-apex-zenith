# Hypertension, Diabetes, and Chronic Disease Danger Signs

## Hypertension danger signs
A known hypertensive patient with severe headache, chest pain, confusion, blurred vision, or
difficulty speaking may be having a hypertensive emergency or stroke and needs immediate referral.
Routine elevated blood pressure without these symptoms should still be followed up with an ANM or
doctor for medication review.

## Diabetes danger signs
Signs of very high blood sugar include excessive thirst, frequent urination, confusion, rapid
breathing, or a fruity-smelling breath — these need urgent referral. Signs of very low blood sugar
(common in patients on diabetes medication) include sudden sweating, shakiness, confusion, or
loss of consciousness; if the patient is conscious and able to swallow, give a sugary drink or food
immediately while arranging transport.

## Stroke warning signs (F.A.S.T.)
Face drooping on one side, Arm weakness on one side, Speech difficulty or slurring, and Time to
call for emergency transport immediately — any one of these signs, even if it resolves quickly,
needs urgent hospital assessment.

## General chronic disease follow-up
Encourage patients with known hypertension, diabetes, or heart disease to continue their prescribed
medications and attend regular follow-up visits, rather than stopping treatment when they feel
well.
