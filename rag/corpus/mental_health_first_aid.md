# Mental Health First Aid

## Recognizing distress
Persistent sadness, withdrawal from usual activities, significant changes in sleep or appetite, or
expressions of hopelessness lasting more than two weeks may indicate depression or another mental
health concern. Approach with empathy, listen without judgment, and encourage the person to seek
support from a health worker or counselor.

## Signs requiring urgent attention
Any expression of wanting to end their life, thoughts of suicide, intent to harm oneself or
others, or a specific plan to do so, is a mental health emergency. Do not leave the person alone,
remove access to obvious means of harm if safe to do so, and arrange urgent referral to a facility
with mental health support.

## Acute agitation or confusion
Sudden severe confusion, agitation, or hallucinations — especially with fever, head injury, or in
someone with a known chronic illness — may have an underlying physical cause and needs prompt
medical assessment rather than being assumed to be purely psychological.

## Supporting caregivers
Caregivers of chronically ill or disabled family members are also at risk of stress and burnout.
Encourage they seek support for themselves as well, and connect them with community resources
where available.
